Confidence intervals in high dimensions, in one R package: SIHR

The R Journal, 2024 · with Prabrisha Rakshit (co-first), T. Tony Cai, Zijian Guo · arXiv:2109.03365

The problem

When a regression has more variables than observations, penalized estimators like the Lasso are the workhorse — but the \(\ell_1\) penalty that makes them work also biases them toward zero. Report a Lasso coefficient with a naive standard error and the resulting confidence interval simply is not valid: it is centered on the wrong place and too short. This bites hardest in exactly the fields that live in high dimensions — genomics, economics, biostatistics — where a point estimate without honest uncertainty is often not enough to make a claim.

The fix: debias, then infer

Take a target like \(x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta\), a prediction at a new covariate value. The plug-in \(x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\widehat\beta\) carries a non-negligible bias; the debiasing correction adds back an estimate of it,

\[ \widehat{x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta} \;=\; x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\widehat\beta \;+\; \widehat u^\top\, \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\big(y_i - X_i^\top\widehat\beta\big), \]

where the projection direction \(\widehat u\) is found by a small constrained program, \(\widehat u = \arg\min_u\, u^\top\widehat\Sigma\, u\) subject to constraints that keep the leftover bias below the noise. The corrected estimator then splits cleanly into a term that is asymptotically normal plus a remaining bias that is provably negligible:

\[ \widehat{x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta} - x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta \;=\; \underbrace{\widehat u^\top \tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_i X_i e_i}_{\text{asymptotically normal}} \;+\; \underbrace{(\widehat\Sigma\widehat u - x_{\mathrm{new}})^\top(\beta - \widehat\beta)}_{\text{negligible bias}}, \]

which hands you an honest confidence interval:

\[ \mathrm{CI} \;=\; \Big(\, \widehat{x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\sqrt{\widehat V}\, \Big). \]

What SIHR packages

The trouble with this machinery is that the corrections are target-specific, fiddly to implement, and scattered across a decade of papers. SIHR puts them behind one consistent interface, for both linear and logistic high-dimensional regression, covering the inference targets that actually come up: individual coefficients; general linear functionals \(x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\beta\); quadratic functionals \(\beta_G^\top A\,\beta_G\) that underpin measures of signal strength and heritability; and two-sample comparisons across models — inner products, distances, and conditional average treatment effects. Each call returns a debiased point estimate with a valid confidence interval, and debiasing is performed only once, so it stays computationally light.

Why it matters

Debiased inference had strong theory but a high implementation barrier — most applied users never got past it. SIHR turns roughly a decade of methodology into a few lines of R, so genomics, economics, and biostatistics workflows can report honest uncertainty in high dimensions instead of point estimates alone. The package is on CRAN, with documentation and worked examples on the website.
