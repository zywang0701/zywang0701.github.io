Classifying reliably in a domain you've never labeled: CG-DRO

Jul 2025 · with Zijian Guo, Yifan Hu, Francis Bach · arXiv:2507.09905

The problem

A hospital wants to deploy a diagnostic classifier on its own patient population. It has richly labeled records from several other hospitals, and plenty of its own patient records — but none of those local records carry the outcome label yet. This is multi-source unsupervised domain adaptation: labeled data from source domains \(1,\dots,L\), and only unlabeled covariates from the target. The domains genuinely differ — both the feature distribution and the way the label depends on features can shift — so a classifier trained on any single source, or naively pooled across all of them, bakes in assumptions about the target that you cannot check without target labels.

The idea

Since the target's label mechanism \(Q_{Y\mid X}\) is unobserved, we refuse to guess it. Instead we hold the (observable) target covariate distribution \(Q_X\) fixed and treat the conditional as some mixture of the sources' conditionals, giving an uncertainty set

\[ \mathcal C \;=\; \Big\{\, (Q_X,\, T_{Y\mid X}) \ :\ T_{Y\mid X} = \textstyle\sum_{l=1}^{L} \gamma_l\, \mathbb P^{(l)}_{Y\mid X},\ \ \gamma \in \Delta_L \,\Big\}. \]

Conditional Group DRO (CG-DRO) then learns the classifier that minimizes the worst-case risk over that set. With the cross-entropy loss \(\ell\), the model \(\theta^\star\) solves

\[ \theta^\star \;=\; \arg\min_{\theta}\ \max_{\gamma \in \Delta_L}\ \sum_{l=1}^{L} \gamma_l\; \mathbb E_{X\sim Q_X}\, \mathbb E_{Y\sim \mathbb P^{(l)}_{Y\mid X}}\, \ell(X, Y, \theta). \]

Whatever the true target mixture turns out to be, the classifier's loss is controlled. This is what separates CG-DRO from classical Group DRO, which mixes the full joint distributions \(\mathbb P^{(l)}\); by keeping \(Q_X\) fixed and mixing only the conditionals, CG-DRO tailors the guarantee to the target covariates we actually observe. When there is no covariate shift the two coincide — but under shift, CG-DRO achieves a strictly lower worst-case risk. Computationally we solve the minimax problem with an efficient Mirror Prox algorithm, and wrap it in double machine learning so that flexible ML estimates of nuisance quantities don't contaminate the final estimator.

The statistical twist

Worst-case optimizers are statistically awkward. When two source mixtures are nearly tied for "worst," the optimal weight \(\gamma^\star\) sits on a boundary of the simplex and the estimator fails to converge to any standard limiting distribution — ordinary normal-approximation confidence intervals are simply invalid. We decouple the randomness into two pieces: a nonstandard component from the weight estimator \(\widehat\gamma\), and a standard Gaussian component from the classifier given the weights. Establishing fast rates through two surrogate minimax problems, we then design a perturbation-based inference procedure that resamples the troublesome component and yields uniformly valid confidence intervals and tests — whether or not the ties actually occur.

Why it matters

CG-DRO delivers both halves of what unsupervised domain adaptation needs: a classifier with a guarantee that holds under distribution shift, and honest uncertainty quantification for it — even in the non-regular regimes where standard asymptotics break down. That combination is what lets a model trained on other hospitals be deployed on your own patients with a confidence interval you can trust.
